06 / AUTONOMY

Where should
automation stop?

A teacher-led model separates AI assistance
from decisions that carry educational responsibility.

Conceptual contrast

Fully autonomous

  1. Request
  2. Generate
  3. Act

When generation and action are collapsed into one flow, educator judgement has little room to intervene.

Design approach

Teacher-led assistance

  1. Teacher sets context
  2. AI retrieves and drafts
  3. HUMAN DECISIONTeacher reviews and edits
  4. Teacher decides use

Match autonomy to the decision.

Explore where assistance ends and educational responsibility takes over.

12 / REFLECTION

What I would
carry forward.

This project changed how I think about AI product design: not as feature automation, but as the design of capability, uncertainty and responsibility.

01

AI quality is a system problem.

Model capability alone does not determine usefulness.

Knowledge quality, context, interaction design and evaluation all shape the final outcome.

02

More automation is not always better design.

The key question is not only what AI can do, but where human judgement and responsibility must remain.

03

Output quality is not learning impact.

A teaching artefact can be evaluated directly.

Improved learning requires longer-term classroom evidence that this project did not yet establish.

If I continued

  1. 01

    Learning-outcome validation

    Connect product evaluation more directly with evidence from real classroom learning.

  2. 02

    Stronger context operationalisation

    Ensure collected teaching parameters meaningfully change generated materials.

  3. 03

    Risk-aware autonomy

    Adjust human oversight according to uncertainty and consequence rather than using one fixed automation rule.

Designing AI products means designing
capabilities, uncertainty and responsibility together.
